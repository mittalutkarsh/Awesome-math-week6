The plan

One way of thinking for every problem this week, in four steps. It rests on the teacher's first picture: pouring the jars into a bowl changes where the beans are, not how many. One running example walks through all four steps.

  1. Draw the slot line: one box per number, smallest to largest.
  2. Turn every clue into a slot fact: mean → Total box, median → middle box, mode → tally.
  3. Fill the boxes: forced values first; then push to an extreme or split into cases.
  4. Check: read every clue back off the finished line.

The Total box

The mean is the equal share, so the Total box always holds mean × count, and you can run it in either direction.

From class. The class opened with exactly these jars (Problem 0.1): empty them into a bowl, share equally, 47 each. The board example then found a tenth test score: ten students average 85, so the Total is 850; the nine known scores make 784, so the tenth is 66.

More worked numbers. Three people average 40 years; two are 25 and 51. Total 120, so the third is 120 − 76 = 44.

Changing the line

When boxes are added, removed or changed, fix the Total box first, then divide by the new count.

From class. The teacher added a sixth jar holding 47 to the jelly beans: (235 + 47) ÷ 6 is still 47. He also added 200 to the list 4, 5, 5, 6, which dragged the mean from 5 to 44 while the median stayed 5.

More worked numbers. Ten numbers average 7 (Total 70). Remove a 16: 54 ÷ 9 = 6.

The middle box

Sort first; an odd line has one middle box, an even line has a middle pair whose average is the median.

From class. Examples 2.1 and 2.2, then the board's even lists 1, 2, 2, 4, 6, 12 and 1, 2, 2, 2, 4, 6. The teacher kept the outlier list 4, 5, 5, 6, 200 to compare with the mean.

More worked numbers. Example 2.2, 1, 7, 19, 13, 16, sorts to 1, 7, 13, 16, 19: box 3 holds 13, not the 19 written in the middle.

Push to the extreme

To make one box as big as possible, make every other box as small as the rules allow, and the other way round.

From class. Median practice 4: 7, y, 18, x, 35 with Total 100, so x + y = 40. Push y down to 8 and x is 32; push y up to 17 and x is 23. The twenty-distinct-integers challenge pushed the tenth number up to 12 at most.

More worked numbers. Three different positive integers have mean 5. The largest is as big as possible when the others are 1 and 2: 15 − 3 = 12.

The tally

The mode is the value with the tallest stack in the tally; “unique mode” means one stack is strictly the tallest.

From class. Example 3.1 (the jars, mode 30) and the board's two-mode list 1, 2, 4, 4, 4, 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 8. The teacher noted that a unique mode forces something to repeat, which often creates cases.

More worked numbers. 3, 5, 3, 7, 9, 1, 9, 2, 3, 4: 3 fills three boxes, 9 fills two, everything else one. Mode 3.

Where can x sit?

When the middle box depends on x, split into cases by where x sits, solve each, and keep x only if it really lands there.

From class. Median practice 3 (3, 5, 11, x, 18, 20 with median 12): the teacher placed x between 11 and 18 because any other place makes the middle pair wrong. The combined board puzzle found x = 7 by asking where x could sit relative to 3.

More worked numbers. 3, 8 and x have equal mean and median. x at the bottom: Total 9, x = −2. x in the middle: 11 + x = 3x, x = 5.5. x at the top: Total 24, x = 13. All three land in their own gaps.

Blocks of boxes

Equal values form blocks; total each block, then divide by all the boxes, not by the number of blocks.

From class. Problem 4.2: women to men 11 : 10, mean ages 34 and 32. The teacher used 11 women and 10 men as a representative group: 374 + 320 = 694 years over 21 people, 694/21 = 33 1/21, not 33.

More worked numbers. Five boxes of 12 and five boxes of 20: equal blocks, so here the plain average (12 + 20) ÷ 2 = 16 happens to be right.

Tricky bits

Each trap comes from a real problem in the set, with the tempting wrong answer crossed out.

  • Forgetting x's box (Problem 2)
    2 × 5 = 10 x is a box too: six boxes, so the Total is 2 × 6.
  • Leaving the last game out (Problem 1)
    88 ÷ 18.5 the season average covers every game, so the Total is 88 + 23.
  • Averaging the old mean with the new box (Problem 7)
    38 stays 38 both removed numbers are above 38, so the Total drops more than its share.
  • Dividing by pairs instead of boxes (Problem 4.1)
    −100 ÷ 100 = −1 the question averages 200 terms; pairs were only for adding.
  • Opposite signs cancel (Problem 4.1)
    the average is 0 1 and −2 don't cancel: each pair leaves −1.
  • Averaging group means (Problem 13)
    (35 + 50) ÷ 2 = 42.5 the groups are different sizes; balance them at 40.
  • Reading the middle before sorting (Problem 10)
    box 5 of the list as written the slot line is always smallest to largest.
  • One middle box for an even count (Board: twenty distinct integers)
    the median is the 10th number twenty boxes have a middle pair, boxes 10 and 11.
  • Putting x only in the middle (Problem 9)
    x = 6 only x could also be the smallest or the largest; each place gives another x.
  • Forgetting the other boxes when pushing (Problem 11)
    42 − 6 = 36 six other boxes must hold something; make them as small as the rules allow.
  • A tie isn't a unique mode (Problem 16)
    6, 6, 9, 9 6 and 9 both appear twice, so 9 isn't the only mode.
  • Mode is the biggest number (Problem 4)
    mode 17 the mode is the value that fills the most boxes.
  • Median must be in the list (Problem 16)
    an odd number of items with an odd count the median is a box, so it would be in the list.
  • Every box +20 means the Total +20 (Problem 5)
    (E) or (C) ten boxes each grow by 20; the Total grows by 200 and the mean by 20.
  • Counting z once (Problem 15)
    (A) correct the student's two-step average counts z twice.

Glossary

Observation (data value)
One entry in the list. Repeated equal entries still count separately: 30, 30 is two boxes.
Set
In these problems, often used loosely for a list that may repeat values. When it really means a set (Problem 9), no value repeats.
Mean (arithmetic mean, average)
Total ÷ count: the equal share.
Total
Everything added up. Total = mean × count.
Median
The middle box of the sorted line, or the average of the middle pair when the count is even.
Mode
A value that fills the most boxes.
Unique mode
Exactly one value fills the most boxes; a tie means there is no unique mode.
Frequency
How many boxes a value fills.
Range
Largest − smallest.
Distinct / different
No two boxes hold the same value.
Positive integer
A whole number bigger than 0: 1, 2, 3, …
Increasing order
Smallest to largest. With distinct values, each box is strictly bigger than the one before.
Outlier
A value far from the rest. It drags the mean but not the median.
Weighted mean
The mean of groups of different sizes: block totals added, divided by every box.
slot line → slot facts → fill → check

Write-up habits

  1. Draw the slot line before writing any equation, sorted smallest to largest, with the positions underneath.
  2. Write the Total box as soon as a mean appears: “Total = mean × count = …”.
  3. Say which box is the middle: “7 boxes → box 4” or “8 boxes → boxes 4 and 5”.
  4. For a mode, draw the tally and say how many boxes the mode fills.
  5. When you push, say what you pushed and why it can't go further (“different integers, so box 2 ≥ 8”).
  6. When you split into cases, give each case its own line and a ✓ or ✗.
  7. Finish by reading every clue back off the finished line.
  8. Fractions and decimals are both fine answers: 3.5 or 7/2.
